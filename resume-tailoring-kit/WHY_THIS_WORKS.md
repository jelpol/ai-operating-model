# Why This Works

One page on the reasoning, so you know what you are running and can explain it to anyone.

## The three filters between you and an interview

Every application passes three different readers, and each one fails resumes for different reasons.

1. **The software (ATS).** Most portals parse your resume into a database before a human sees it. Parsers reward exact keyword matches against the job description. A resume that says "led incident handling" when the posting says "incident response" can lose the match. This kit's answer: module 02 checks that every must-have keyword appears verbatim in the ATS version, and module 04 builds a separate ATS file formatted for parsing, distinct from the pretty PRINT version humans read.

2. **The recruiter (6 seconds).** First human contact is a skim: title match, seniority match, location, red flags. This kit's answer: the QA gate (module 06) simulates that skim as its own review pass, and the content build enforces a bullet rubric (Action, Scope, Outcome, Evidence, Tech or Method, 40 words maximum) so every line lands fast.

3. **The hiring manager (the real read).** This reader has done the job and smells inflation instantly. Overclaim one thing and every other line gets discounted. This kit's answer: the fact registry. Claims trace to confirmed facts with evidence, scoping language stays honest ("contributed to" is not "owned"), and module 07 audits the final draft line by line before anything ships.

## Why the fact registry beats "just make my resume better"

Asking an AI to improve a resume produces confident, polished fiction. The registry inverts the workflow: the AI's job is never to invent your experience, only to select, phrase, and order facts you already confirmed. Three effects follow.

1. Interviews get easier. Every line on the page has a story behind it that you already told once, in your own words, with numbers.
2. Tailoring gets faster. After the first build, each new job is a re-selection from the same registry, not a rewrite from scratch.
3. Gaps become visible. When a job requires something the registry cannot support, the scoring module shows the honest number, and the interrogation module checks whether you have unrecorded experience that closes it. Sometimes you do. When you do not, the qualification module tells you to stop spending evenings on that target.

## Why target qualification is in the pipeline at all

The most expensive mistake in a job search is not a bad resume; it is weeks of effort aimed at roles you would not take or cannot get. Module 13 forces each posting through your own written career thesis before any build effort is spent. A do-not-pursue verdict two minutes into a run is the system saving you a night of work.

## A note on ATS claims

The ATS guidance in this kit (verbatim keywords, parse-safe formatting, portal notes) is practical heuristics from real submissions, not a description of how every system works. Parsing software varies and changes; treat the guidance as odds-improvement, never as a guarantee, and update it when your own results disagree.

## What this kit will not do

It will not fabricate experience, pad titles, or promise interviews. It raises the odds that the experience you actually have gets seen, parsed, and believed. The rest is the market and you.
